Executive Overview
Purpose

A connected commerce experience, not only an online catalogue

The platform supports the full customer lifecycle: attracting shoppers, helping them make a confident product decision, completing payment, tracking fulfilment, and re-engaging them after purchase.

The central experience promise is simple: make electronics easier to discover, safer to buy, and more valuable to own.

Journey Detail
Stages 4-6

From cart to delivery and the next purchase

Conversion is not complete at payment. A reliable post-purchase experience protects trust and creates the permission to market to the customer again.

Step 1Cart review

Customer checks items, images, quantities, variants, pricing, tax, shipping and the free-shipping progress message.

Step 2Customer details

Profile data can reduce repeat entry. Address, city, state, phone and pincode are validated before payment.

Step 3Offer and payment

Coupon eligibility is checked and the customer chooses secure online payment through Razorpay or cash on delivery.

Step 4Confirmation

The order record, payment status, customer summary and items are confirmed with a unique order reference.

Step 5Tracking

Customers can follow progress from ordered and processing through shipping, transit and delivery.

Step 6Re-engagement

Relevant follow-ups can use purchase history, cart, wishlist, activity and consent to select the right message.

C

Conversion controls

Coupon validation, clear totals, payment choices, delivery checks and error feedback reduce avoidable exits.

T

Trust controls

Server-side pricing, payment verification, authenticated accounts and status visibility protect the transaction.

R

Retention controls

Consent-aware profiles, preference management and relevance rules protect the customer relationship.

Operational dependency: cart recovery, wishlist alerts and lifecycle reminder logic exist, but scheduled execution must be connected to Vercel Cron or another secure scheduler before these journeys run automatically.

User Experience
Experience Framework

Six principles for a consistent user experience

These principles should guide storefront changes, campaign landing pages, account features and customer communication.

01

Clarity before persuasion

Show product, price, discount, availability, delivery and terms before asking for commitment.

02

Progressive information

Lead with decision essentials, then provide specifications and policies for users who need depth.

03

Mobile-first action

Keep search, filters, cart and primary purchase actions reachable on smaller screens.

04

Visible system status

Use loading, success, error, stock, payment and order states so users always know what happened.

05

Trust through consistency

Use consistent brand language, totals, policies, security signals and post-purchase communication.

06

Relevant personalisation

Use behaviour and consent to reduce noise, not to create intrusive or unexplained experiences.

Experience quality checklist

Primary action is clear on every key page
Prices and discounts remain consistent through checkout
Errors explain how the customer can recover
Forms request only information needed for the task
Mobile layouts preserve essential product information
Images have useful alternative text
Keyboard focus and contrast remain visible
Consent and unsubscribe choices are respected
Campaign pages match the promise in the advertisement
Order and payment states use plain language
Slow network states do not appear as failure
Support and store contact paths are easy to find
Brand tone: confident, clear, modern and approachable. Avoid technical jargon in customer-facing messages and explain the benefit before the feature.

The customer marketing system

The setup should connect customer signals to decisions, messages, destinations and measurement.

1. Data and identity

Use account, profile, order, cart, wishlist, campaign and preference data. Identity must come from the authenticated session; consent status governs communication eligibility.

2. Segmentation

Create mutually understandable segments such as new, active, high-value, dormant, cart-intent, wishlist-intent and category-interest. Document entry and exit rules.

3. Content and offer

Maintain reusable templates for new products, promotional codes, major sales, festivals and custom messages. Keep product and offer claims consistent with the destination page.

4. Orchestration

Define trigger, delay, channel, frequency cap, exclusion and stop condition. Scheduled journeys require a secure external scheduler and cron secret.

5. Delivery channels

Use on-site merchandising for all visitors, email for rich lifecycle content and WhatsApp for consented, timely communication. Do not send the same message everywhere by default.

6. Measurement and learning

Track campaign identity from message to destination and order. Compare against a baseline or control group where practical, then record the learning for the next campaign.

Minimum viable setup: one agreed segment dictionary, one campaign naming standard, one consent policy, one measurement dashboard and one accountable owner per campaign.

ImmediatelyTransactional

Confirmation, payment, delivery and service messages. These must be accurate, timely and separate from promotion.

Marketing Setup
Channels and Measurement

Choose the right channel and measure the full outcome

Channel choice should depend on urgency, content depth, customer preference and consent.

On-site

Best for: merchandising, broad promotions, contextual offers and product education.

Tools: hero slides, home sections, banners, campaign pages, offers and featured products.

Measure: engagement, product views, add-to-cart and conversion.

Email

Best for: rich campaigns, product stories, lifecycle content and less urgent reminders.

Tools: templates, recipient selection, segments, batch sending and campaign history.

Measure: delivery, click, conversion, revenue and unsubscribe.

WhatsApp

Best for: consented, timely and concise communication with a clear action.

Tools: AskEva integration used by notification workflows; configuration is required.

Measure: delivery, response, conversion and opt-out.

Recommended Roadmap
Activation Plan

A practical four-phase rollout

Start with foundations and one measurable journey. Expand only after delivery, consent and reporting are reliable.

Phase 1Foundation

Goal: make data, channels and ownership trustworthy.

Confirm sender configuration, WhatsApp setup, consent rules, campaign naming, analytics events, segment definitions, product data quality and responsible owners.

Phase 2Conversion

Goal: improve the existing buying journey.

Review top landing pages, product detail clarity, coupon experience, checkout errors, payment success, mobile usability and campaign-to-page consistency.

Phase 3Retention pilot

Goal: activate one controlled lifecycle journey.

Connect the scheduler, run cart recovery in dry-run mode, validate exclusions and frequency, enable a limited audience, then measure recovered revenue and opt-outs.

Phase 4Scale

Goal: add journeys without adding noise.

Introduce wishlist alerts, lifecycle reminders, high-value recognition, dormant reactivation, seasonal campaigns and controlled experimentation.

Definition of success

Customer

Less effort, greater confidence and useful communication.

Marketing

Repeatable campaigns with attributable outcomes.

Operations

Clear ownership, reliable execution and visible failures.

Business

Higher conversion, retention and lifetime value without harming trust.

Recommended first action: complete the marketing foundation checklist, then launch one cart-recovery pilot with a defined audience, dry run, frequency cap and revenue dashboard.
