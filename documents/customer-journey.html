One shopping journey. Two checkout choices.

The customer can sign in for saved addresses and account order history, or buy as a guest and keep a private order link. Both paths lead to order confirmation; cash on delivery does not mean the order has already been paid.

01

Discover the store

Home /

The journey starts with a need: a television, appliance or another electronics product. The homepage gives the customer a search box, categories, deals and brand shortcuts instead of requiring them to know an exact product URL.

Customer action
Browse a category or search for a product, then open a promising result.
What the store provides
Product cards with prices, savings and quick shopping actions.

Verified: mobile search found the synthetic catalogue product. The homepage was also assessed at all five audit viewport sizes.

02

Create an account

/register
Account path / optional for guest checkout

A new customer enters their name and email, chooses a password, confirms it and completes the required consent. The form explains how their details are used.

Customer action
Complete the form and follow the verification link sent to their email.
What the store provides
Validation for missing details and consent before the account can be completed.

Verified: missing-field rejection, completed registration and verification through a captured local email link.

What this screenshot does not prove

This is the initial registration form, not the submitted success state. The audit used a local email sink and captcha fixture; real inbox delivery and Google captcha behavior still need hosted testing.

03

Sign in and continue shopping

/login

A returning or newly verified customer signs in with their email and password. The actual signed session, not an old browser hint, determines whether they are logged in.

Customer action
Enter valid credentials and select Sign in.
What the store provides
Access to their profile, saved addresses and orders. An incorrect password is rejected without creating a session.

Verified: wrong-password rejection, successful login, persistent profile changes, logout and protected-page denial.

04

Evaluate the product

/product/audit-fixture-television

The customer opens a product to review its details and price before buying. In the illustrated example, the synthetic television costs INR 4,999.

Customer action
Review the product, optionally save or compare alternatives, then select Add to Cart.
What the store provides
A product-detail page and a mobile purchase bar that keeps the price and cart action available.

Verified: wishlist add/remove persistence, two-product comparison and clearing, and adding a product through the mobile UI.

05

Review the basket and total

/cart

The cart is the customer's last product-level check: is this the right item, quantity and displayed total? They can remove an item or move it to the wishlist instead of continuing.

Customer action
Adjust quantity, review the price breakdown and select Proceed to Checkout.
What the store provides
Quantity controls, savings, delivery charges and a clear progression from cart to address, payment and order placement.

Verified: quantity increment/decrement and the populated mobile cart. Checkout recalculates prices on the server; the displayed browser total is not the payment authority.

06

Provide delivery details

Checkout / saved addresses at /dashboard/addresses

At checkout, the customer supplies their contact and delivery details and chooses an available delivery or pickup option. Signed-in customers can save an address for later use; guests supply their details for that purchase.

Customer action
Complete the address and select the appropriate fulfillment option.
What the store provides
Address validation before payment and saved-address access for an authenticated account.

Verified: missing-address rejection, saving a delivery address and its actual persistence in the isolated database.

07

Choose how to pay

/checkout

The customer chooses a payment option after providing the required delivery information. Online payment and cash on delivery have different confirmation rules; a cancelled or invalid payment must not become a successful paid order.

Signed-in online-payment path

The application prepares a server-priced payment and verifies its proof before creating the paid order. Cancellation preserved the cart; invalid proof was rejected; simulated captured payment created the INR 4,999 order.

Guest cash-on-delivery path

The guest selects COD and places an order without paying online. The separate desktop journey purchased the second synthetic television for INR 6,999, with payment pending on delivery.

Online-payment test boundary

The real application pricing, verification and persistence code ran against a local Razorpay provider and synthetic widget. No real money moved. Genuine hosted payment, UPI/bank behavior, webhook delivery, refunds and reconciliation were not certified.

08

Receive an order confirmation

Post-checkout confirmation
Screenshot: guest COD branch

The confirmation brings together the order number, purchased item, total, delivery details and payment method. The guest should save the private order link so they can return without creating an account.

Customer action
Check the summary and keep the order reference or private link.
What the store provides
An order-placement acknowledgement and progress indicators. For this COD example, payment remains due on delivery and later fulfillment stages await updates.

Verified: guest order persistence, cart cleanup, private-link refresh and denial after removing the stored guest capability. The authenticated simulated online purchase was also verified separately.

09

Return to My Orders

/account/orders and /dashboard/orders

A signed-in customer returns to order history to find their order, open its details or use Track Order. Guests use their private link instead of another customer's account history.

Customer action
Open My Orders and select the relevant order.
What the store provides
Order reference, item, amount and current status in one place.

Verified: the authenticated online purchase appeared in history and the cart was empty. Account/dashboard route views passed at five sizes.

10

Get help before or after buying

Contact enquiries / complaints

The journey continues when a customer needs advice or has a problem. They can send an enquiry, or submit a complaint with the required image and retain its reference for follow-up.

Customer action
Provide the requested details and consent, add supporting information where required, then submit.
What the store provides
A visible acknowledgement rather than leaving the customer unsure whether their message was received.

Verified: enquiry submission with consent, complaint image validation/upload and stored reference, and newsletter acknowledgement. Database assertions confirmed the records were saved.

+

If the customer forgets their password

/forgot-password → /reset-password → /login
Recovery branch

Forgetting a password should not end the shopping journey. The customer requests a recovery email, follows its reset link, sets a new password and signs in again.

Customer action
Select Forgot password on the login form and follow the recovery steps.
What the store provides
A reset request screen and a token-based password-reset flow.

Verified: the recovery email was captured locally, its link was used in the browser, and login with the new password succeeded.
